# Open Tinnitus Lab

**Open, AI-assisted research into the causes and mechanisms of tinnitus.**

[![License: Apache 2.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-Apache%202.0-blue.svg)](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)
[![Status: Research](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-research-orange.svg)]()

## Mission

**Open Tinnitus Lab** is an open scientific research project investigating the biological mechanisms underlying tinnitus.

Our long-term objective is ambitious:

> **Understand why tinnitus occurs, identify its underlying mechanisms and subtypes, and help discover effective ways to prevent, reverse, or treat it.**

The project combines:

* scientific literature
* computational analysis
* artificial intelligence
* data science
* systems biology
* neuroscience
* open collaboration
* patient and public participation
* experimental research

The project is deliberately **open, evidence-driven and hypothesis-neutral**.

We are not starting with a predetermined explanation for tinnitus.

We are starting with a question:

> **What do we actually know about why tinnitus occurs, what don't we know, and what experiments would allow us to find out?**

---

## Why this project?

Tinnitus affects a large global population, yet its underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood.

"Tinnitus" is also not necessarily a single disease.

People with the same reported symptom can have very different:

* triggers
* hearing profiles
* neurological characteristics
* tinnitus sounds
* symptom patterns
* responses to interventions
* associated conditions

Existing research has proposed numerous mechanisms, including changes in cochlear function, auditory nerve activity, central auditory gain, neural synchronisation, predictive processing, attention, salience networks, somatosensory interactions and autonomic processes.

Many of these mechanisms have supporting evidence.

Many also have contradictory evidence.

The challenge is therefore not simply to collect more information.

It is to determine:

1. Which findings are robust?
2. Which findings have been independently replicated?
3. Which findings contradict one another?
4. Which proposed mechanisms are actually causal?
5. Are there biologically distinct tinnitus subtypes?
6. What predictions does each mechanism make?
7. Which experiments could distinguish competing explanations?
8. Which interventions could potentially reverse the underlying mechanism?

# A different approach to scientific research

Open Tinnitus Lab is intended to function as a **digital research laboratory** rather than a conventional information website.

The project will develop an evidence system in which scientific claims can be connected to their supporting and contradicting evidence.

For example:

```text
Noise exposure
      │
      ▼
Cochlear injury
      │
      ▼
Reduced auditory input
      │
      ▼
Central compensation
      │
      ▼
Altered neural activity
      │
      ▼
Tinnitus perception
```

Instead of treating this as an established causal chain, the laboratory asks:

> **Which of these relationships have actually been demonstrated?**

Each relationship should eventually be associated with:

* supporting studies
* contradictory studies
* study populations
* experimental methods
* sample sizes
* effect sizes where available
* limitations
* replication status
* confidence
* unresolved questions

The objective is to create a **living evidence map of tinnitus research**.

# AI as a research instrument

AI will be used extensively, but it will not be treated as an authority.

AI systems may assist with:

* literature discovery
* paper classification
* evidence extraction
* identifying contradictions
* knowledge-graph construction
* statistical analysis
* computational modelling
* hypothesis generation
* experiment design
* code generation
* dataset analysis
* replication analysis
* scientific writing

AI-generated claims will always be distinguishable from experimentally or independently established scientific findings.

A central principle of the project is:

> **AI generates possibilities. Evidence determines what survives.**

# Research methodology

The project will follow a progressively stronger evidence pipeline:

```text
Observation
    ↓
Literature
    ↓
Evidence extraction
    ↓
Mechanistic model
    ↓
Hypothesis
    ↓
Prediction
    ↓
Computational test
    ↓
Experimental test
    ↓
Independent replication
    ↓
Peer review
    ↓
Scientific finding
```

A hypothesis is not considered a scientific finding simply because an AI system considers it plausible.

---

# Hypothesis-driven research

The laboratory will maintain an open registry of hypotheses.

Each hypothesis will have a unique identifier and a transparent history.

For example:

```text
H-0001

Title:
Central auditory gain is a causal mechanism in a defined
subtype of chronic tinnitus following acoustic trauma.

Status:
Unresolved

Evidence supporting:
...

Evidence contradicting:
...

Predictions:
...

Failed predictions:
...

Potential experiments:
...

Last updated:
...
```

The project will actively seek evidence **against** its own hypotheses.

This is a core design principle.

> **A hypothesis becomes more interesting when attempts to falsify it fail.**

---

# Contradictions are evidence

Most scientific literature systems are optimized for finding supporting evidence.

Open Tinnitus Lab will explicitly search for contradictions.

For every major proposed mechanism we will ask:

> What evidence would we expect to see if this mechanism were correct?

and:

> Has that evidence actually been observed?

and:

> What observations cannot be explained by this mechanism?

Contradictory findings will not be discarded simply because they are inconvenient.

They will be treated as potential clues to:

* incorrect theories
* experimental limitations
* population differences
* tinnitus subtypes
* hidden variables
* competing mechanisms

---

# Tinnitus subtypes

A major research question is whether tinnitus represents a collection of biologically distinct conditions rather than one disease.

The project will investigate whether differences in:

* onset
* hearing loss
* acoustic exposure
* tinnitus characteristics
* somatosensory modulation
* neurological measurements
* sleep
* medication exposure
* autonomic state
* response to treatment

can identify meaningful biological subgroups.

If multiple mechanisms exist, the ultimate goal may not be a single "cure for tinnitus".

It may instead be:

> **Identify the mechanism responsible for an individual's tinnitus and target that mechanism specifically.**

# Human participation

The project welcomes contributions from:

* scientists
* clinicians
* researchers
* programmers
* statisticians
* engineers
* students
* people with tinnitus
* citizen scientists
* science communicators

No formal scientific qualification is required to contribute to the open project.

# Patient data and privacy

Open Tinnitus Lab may eventually support voluntary contribution of health and tinnitus-related data.

Any such work must prioritize:

* informed consent
* privacy
* anonymisation
* data minimisation
* secure storage
* appropriate ethical oversight
* applicable data-protection law
* participant control over data where possible

**No personally identifiable medical information should be committed to this repository.**

Patient datasets will require a separate data-governance framework before collection begins.

---

# What this project is not

Open Tinnitus Lab is **not**:

* a medical treatment service
* a replacement for medical professionals
* a source of individualized medical advice
* a platform for promoting unproven tinnitus treatments
* an AI-generated medical advice website
* a claim that tinnitus already has a known single cause
* a guarantee that the project will discover a cure

Experimental treatments should never be undertaken on the basis of an AI-generated hypothesis.

Human studies and interventions require appropriate clinical, ethical and regulatory oversight.

# Research principles

The project will follow several core principles.

### 1. Evidence over authority

A claim should be supported by evidence, not by reputation.

### 2. Falsifiability

Important hypotheses must make predictions that could prove them wrong.

### 3. Reproducibility

Computational results should be reproducible whenever possible.

### 4. Transparency

Research decisions and changes in interpretation should be documented.

### 5. Negative results matter

Failed hypotheses and failed experiments are scientifically valuable.

### 6. Independent verification

Important findings should be independently replicated.

### 7. AI is not evidence

AI-generated reasoning does not constitute scientific evidence.

### 8. Patient safety comes first

No experimental intervention should be undertaken without appropriate scientific and ethical oversight.

### 9. Open collaboration

Useful contributions should be accessible to the wider research community whenever legally and ethically possible.

### 10. Follow the evidence

The project must remain willing to abandon attractive theories when the evidence contradicts them.

# Initial research program

The first phase of Open Tinnitus Lab will focus on constructing a rigorous evidence base.

## Phase 1 — Literature

Identify and classify foundational tinnitus research.

## Phase 2 — Evidence extraction

Extract individual scientific claims rather than relying solely on paper-level summaries.

## Phase 3 — Evidence graph

Connect claims, mechanisms, observations and contradictions.

## Phase 4 — Mechanism map

Construct competing causal models of tinnitus.

## Phase 5 — Contradiction analysis

Identify findings that existing models cannot adequately explain.

## Phase 6 — Hypothesis generation

Generate novel, testable hypotheses from unresolved evidence gaps.

## Phase 7 — Prediction

Determine what each hypothesis predicts that competing hypotheses do not.

## Phase 8 — Computational testing

Test hypotheses against existing datasets wherever possible.

## Phase 9 — Experimental collaboration

Identify laboratories capable of testing the strongest hypotheses.

## Phase 10 — Independent validation

Seek replication and peer-reviewed publication.

# Long-term vision

The long-term goal is not simply to build another tinnitus information resource.

It is to build an **open scientific research infrastructure** capable of helping humans investigate one of the most poorly understood neurological/auditory conditions.

The ultimate measure of success is not the sophistication of the AI.

It is whether the project helps produce **new, reproducible scientific knowledge**.

And, ultimately:

> **A better understanding of tinnitus that leads to better prevention and treatment.**
