# Open Tinnitus Lab — Research Questions

## Primary research question

> **What biological mechanisms cause or maintain chronic subjective tinnitus, and how can those mechanisms be distinguished, tested and ultimately targeted?**

This question is intentionally broad.

The initial research program will progressively narrow it based on evidence.

---

# Research Area 1 — Definition

### RQ-001

What exactly is meant by "tinnitus" in the scientific literature?

### RQ-002

Which tinnitus characteristics can be measured reliably?

### RQ-003

Which apparently different tinnitus phenotypes may represent biologically different conditions?

---

# Research Area 2 — Onset

### RQ-004

What biological events immediately precede the onset of tinnitus?

### RQ-005

Why do some people develop persistent tinnitus after an apparent trigger while others do not?

### RQ-006

Why does tinnitus sometimes arise without an obvious initiating event?

---

# Research Area 3 — Peripheral auditory mechanisms

### RQ-007

What role does cochlear damage play in tinnitus?

### RQ-008

What role does cochlear synaptopathy play?

### RQ-009

Can tinnitus occur despite apparently normal conventional audiometry?

### RQ-010

Which peripheral measurements best predict tinnitus?

---

# Research Area 4 — Central auditory mechanisms

### RQ-011

Does reduced auditory input cause compensatory increases in central auditory gain?

### RQ-012

Is increased spontaneous neural activity causally involved in tinnitus?

### RQ-013

Is abnormal neural synchronisation necessary for tinnitus?

### RQ-014

Which auditory brain regions are causally involved?

---

# Research Area 5 — Distributed brain networks

### RQ-015

What roles do attention and salience networks play?

### RQ-016

What role does the limbic system play?

### RQ-017

Are changes in auditory processing sufficient to produce conscious tinnitus, or are broader brain networks required?

---

# Research Area 6 — Somatosensory mechanisms

### RQ-018

Why can tinnitus sometimes be changed by movement of the jaw, head or neck?

### RQ-019

What neural pathways connect somatosensory systems with auditory processing?

### RQ-020

Does somatosensory modulation identify a biologically distinct tinnitus subtype?

---

# Research Area 7 — Autonomic and physiological mechanisms

### RQ-021

What relationship exists between tinnitus and autonomic nervous-system activity?

### RQ-022

Do sleep, stress and physiological arousal modify tinnitus through measurable biological pathways?

### RQ-023

Could autonomic state be a causal contributor rather than merely a response to tinnitus?

---

# Research Area 8 — Individual variability

### RQ-024

Why do people with apparently similar hearing damage experience different tinnitus outcomes?

### RQ-025

Why do some people with significant hearing damage never develop persistent tinnitus?

### RQ-026

Can measurable characteristics predict who will develop chronic tinnitus?

---

# Research Area 9 — Natural history

### RQ-027

Why does tinnitus become persistent in some people but resolve in others?

### RQ-028

What biological changes occur when tinnitus spontaneously decreases or disappears?

### RQ-029

Can spontaneous remission reveal mechanisms responsible for tinnitus persistence?

---

# Research Area 10 — Treatment response

### RQ-030

Why do some individuals respond strongly to particular interventions while others do not?

### RQ-031

Can treatment response identify biological subtypes?

### RQ-032

Which interventions appear to modify the underlying mechanism rather than merely changing the perception or distress associated with tinnitus?

---

# Research Area 11 — Causality

### RQ-033

Which proposed tinnitus mechanisms have actual causal evidence?

### RQ-034

Which widely repeated tinnitus mechanisms are primarily supported by correlation?

### RQ-035

Which causal experiments would most efficiently distinguish competing mechanisms?

---

# Research Area 12 — Discovery

### RQ-036

What important tinnitus observations are currently unexplained?

### RQ-037

Which contradictions in the literature cannot be explained by known methodological differences?

### RQ-038

What predictions made by existing tinnitus theories have never been adequately tested?

### RQ-039

Can computational analysis identify previously unrecognized relationships in existing tinnitus datasets?

### RQ-040

Can those relationships generate novel, falsifiable biological hypotheses?

---

# Initial priority

The first research task is **not** to answer all of these questions.

The initial objective is to determine:

> **Which questions have already been answered convincingly, which remain unresolved, and which contain the most important contradictions or research gaps?**

This will be determined from the scientific literature rather than assumed in advance.

---

# Research question lifecycle

Each research question may progress through:

```text
Proposed
   ↓
Literature review
   ↓
Evidence mapped
   ↓
Resolved / Partially resolved / Unresolved
   ↓
Hypothesis generated
   ↓
Prediction identified
   ↓
Test proposed
   ↓
Test performed
   ↓
Conclusion
```

Questions should remain open when evidence is insufficient.

---

# Guiding question

The project should repeatedly return to:

> **What do we know, how do we know it, what contradicts it, and what experiment would most efficiently tell us what is true?**
