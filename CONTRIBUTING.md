# Contributing to Open Tinnitus Lab

Thank you for your interest in contributing.

Open Tinnitus Lab is intended to be an open research project involving scientists, clinicians, programmers, statisticians, engineers, students, people with tinnitus and other interested contributors.

You do not need to be a professional scientist to contribute.

However, scientific claims are evaluated according to the quality of their evidence rather than the credentials of the contributor.

---

# Ways to contribute

## Scientific literature

Help identify:

* important papers
* replication studies
* contradictory findings
* systematic reviews
* clinical trials
* relevant datasets

When possible, provide:

* DOI
* PubMed ID
* journal
* publication year

---

## Evidence extraction

Help extract structured information from papers.

Examples:

* population
* sample size
* methodology
* measurements
* findings
* statistical results
* limitations
* conclusions

---

## Scientific criticism

Challenge existing interpretations.

Useful contributions include:

* methodological criticisms
* alternative explanations
* contradictory evidence
* replication failures
* missing controls
* statistical concerns

---

## Programming

Potential areas include:

* literature ingestion
* APIs
* databases
* AI agents
* natural-language processing
* statistical analysis
* visualisation
* knowledge graphs
* reproducible research tools

---

## Data science

Potential contributions include:

* dataset cleaning
* statistical analysis
* modelling
* machine learning
* causal inference
* reproducibility studies

---

## Research questions

Anyone can propose a research question.

A good research question should be:

* specific
* answerable
* measurable
* scientifically meaningful

---

## Hypotheses

Novel hypotheses are welcome.

However, hypotheses should clearly distinguish:

* existing evidence
* inference
* speculation

AI-generated hypotheses should be explicitly labelled.

---

# Contribution principles

### Be constructive

Criticism should focus on evidence and methodology.

### Cite sources

Scientific claims should have traceable sources.

### Do not fabricate evidence

Never invent:

* papers
* citations
* experimental results
* datasets
* statistics

### Preserve uncertainty

If something is unknown, say that it is unknown.

### Do not overstate conclusions

Correlation should not be described as causation.

### Document AI assistance

Where AI materially contributes to analysis or content, the contribution should be recorded.

---

# Pull requests

For code contributions:

1. Create a branch.
2. Make the change.
3. Explain the purpose.
4. Include tests where appropriate.
5. Submit a pull request.

For scientific contributions:

1. Identify the claim or question.
2. Provide supporting sources.
3. Explain the reasoning.
4. Identify uncertainty and limitations.
5. Submit the contribution for review.

---

# Scientific review

Important scientific contributions may undergo additional review before being incorporated into the evidence base.

Review may involve:

* another contributor
* a subject-matter expert
* an independent analysis
* primary-source verification

The project may use AI to assist review, but AI review does not replace human scientific judgement.

---

# Patient contributions

Personal experience is welcome and may identify important research questions.

However, personal experience should not be represented as evidence that a particular treatment or mechanism works generally.

Contributors should never submit personally identifiable health information to the public repository.

---

# Medical safety

Do not use this repository to request or provide instructions for unsafe self-experimentation.

The project is intended to generate and evaluate scientific knowledge, not to facilitate unsupervised medical experimentation.

---

# Code of conduct

Contributors are expected to:

* act in good faith
* respect other contributors
* distinguish disagreement from personal criticism
* acknowledge uncertainty
* correct mistakes when identified
* prioritize scientific accuracy over winning an argument

---

# The most valuable contribution

One of the most valuable things a contributor can say is:

> **"I don't think this is correct, and here is the evidence showing why."**

Scientific progress depends on being able to change our minds.
