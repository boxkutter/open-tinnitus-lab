# Open Tinnitus Lab — Governance

## Purpose

Open Tinnitus Lab is an open research project investigating the causes, mechanisms and potential treatments of tinnitus.

The project uses artificial intelligence, computational methods, scientific literature, open collaboration and, where appropriate, experimental research.

This document defines how the project is governed and how scientific decisions are made.

---

## 1. Core objective

The objective of the laboratory is to produce **reproducible scientific knowledge**.

The project is not intended to prove a predetermined theory, promote a particular treatment, or validate the assumptions of its contributors.

The project must remain willing to change or abandon its conclusions when the evidence changes.

---

## 2. Scientific independence

Open Tinnitus Lab will attempt to remain independent of:

* pharmaceutical companies
* medical-device manufacturers
* supplement companies
* tinnitus treatment providers
* political organizations
* advocacy organizations with a predetermined scientific position

Funding or collaboration from external organizations may be considered, but funding must not determine scientific conclusions.

Any material conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

---

## 3. Evidence before interpretation

The project distinguishes between:

1. **Observation** — something directly measured or reported.
2. **Finding** — a result supported by an analysis or experiment.
3. **Interpretation** — an explanation proposed for a finding.
4. **Hypothesis** — a testable proposed explanation.
5. **Causal claim** — a claim that changing one variable causes a change in another.
6. **Scientific conclusion** — a conclusion supported by sufficient evidence and appropriate validation.

These categories must not be conflated.

---

## 4. Role of artificial intelligence

AI systems are research instruments, not scientific authorities.

AI may be used to:

* discover literature
* extract information
* classify evidence
* identify contradictions
* generate hypotheses
* design analyses
* write code
* analyze datasets
* propose experiments
* identify research gaps

AI-generated output must never automatically become accepted evidence.

Important AI-generated claims must be independently verified against primary sources or reproducible analyses.

---

## 5. Human responsibility

Humans remain responsible for:

* scientific decisions
* interpretation of evidence
* research ethics
* data governance
* publication decisions
* clinical safety
* external communications
* approval of important changes to research conclusions

No AI system has authority to approve human experimentation or clinical intervention.

---

## 6. Scientific criticism

Criticism is considered a normal and necessary part of the project.

Contributors are encouraged to challenge:

* hypotheses
* evidence classifications
* statistical methods
* interpretations
* conclusions
* AI-generated reasoning
* project methodology

Good-faith scientific disagreement must not be suppressed because it conflicts with the current project consensus.

---

## 7. Conflicts of interest

Contributors should disclose relevant conflicts of interest when contributing to scientific conclusions.

Examples include:

* financial interests
* employment
* patents
* commercial treatment interests
* ownership of relevant companies
* involvement in studies being evaluated
* personal relationships that could materially affect scientific judgement

Personal experience with tinnitus is not itself considered a conflict of interest.

---

## 8. Research record

Important scientific decisions should be recorded.

Where practical, the project should preserve:

* previous hypotheses
* previous evidence assessments
* changes in interpretation
* negative results
* failed analyses
* retracted or corrected papers
* disagreements
* methodological changes

The goal is to preserve the history of how conclusions were reached.

---

## 9. Negative results

Negative findings are scientifically valuable.

The project will not selectively publish only results that support its hypotheses.

Failed predictions, failed experiments and contradictory evidence should be retained wherever legally and ethically possible.

---

## 10. Publication

The project may publish:

* research reports
* datasets
* software
* preprints
* peer-reviewed papers
* methodological papers
* evidence reviews
* computational analyses

Peer-reviewed publication is preferred for claims presented as scientific findings.

Preprints and working papers must be clearly identified as not yet peer reviewed.

---

## 11. Patient involvement

People with tinnitus are encouraged to participate in research-question generation, evidence review, usability testing and other appropriate activities.

Patient experience can identify important research questions.

However, individual experience must not automatically be treated as scientific evidence of causation or treatment efficacy.

---

## 12. Human research

The project will not independently conduct human experiments, administer experimental treatments, or recommend unvalidated interventions on the basis of AI-generated reasoning.

Human research must use appropriate:

* ethical review
* informed consent
* clinical oversight
* safety procedures
* regulatory processes
* data protection

The project may develop hypotheses and experimental proposals for qualified researchers to evaluate.

---

## 13. Governance evolution

This document is an initial governance framework.

As the project grows, governance may evolve to include:

* a scientific advisory board
* clinical advisors
* patient representatives
* independent reviewers
* data-governance officers
* institutional research partners

Changes to governance should be documented publicly.

---

## 14. Guiding principle

The laboratory follows one overriding principle:

> **The purpose of the project is to find out what is true, not to prove that we are right.**
